## Do is the home note, and it moves

In tonic solfa the scale is always do, re, mi, fa, so, la, ti, and do is the
home note of the song. That home note can be any key. When a song is "in G",
do is G. When it is in D, do is D. This is called movable do, and it is why
the same solfa fits a song in any key.

Written tonic solfa often shortens the syllables to letters: d r m f s l t.
Older books spell them doh, ray, me, fah, soh, lah, te. They all mean the same
seven notes.

## Where each syllable falls in common keys

Find your key on the left. The row shows which key to press for each syllable.

| do = | do | re | mi | fa | so | la | ti |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | C | D | E | F | G | A | B |
| G | G | A | B | C | D | E | F# |
| D | D | E | F# | G | A | B | C# |
| A | A | B | C# | D | E | F# | G# |
| F | F | G | A | B♭ (A#) | C | D | E |
| B♭ (A#) | B♭ (A#) | C | D | E♭ (D#) | F | G | A |

In C every syllable is a white key, which is why many players learn a song in
C first. In the [song to tonic solfa](/song-to-tonic-solfa) player, "Moved so
do = C" shows any song's key names as if it were in C.

## High and low notes

The same syllable can be played in different octaves. A mark after the
syllable moves it: an apostrophe for higher, a comma for lower. So do' is the
do above the usual one, and so, is the so below it. The player also writes the
exact key, such as G3 or G4, under every syllable.

## Sharps, flats and minor songs

- A note between two scale notes gets a changed vowel. Raised notes end in -i:
  di, ri, fi, si, li. Fi is the black key just above fa.
- In minor songs the home note is la, not do. A song in A minor is written
  with do = C and centres on la. The raised so, si, is common in minor tunes.

## Note lengths

Printed tonic solfa marks beats with colons and bar lines, and a dash means
hold the note. The player shows each note's length as a short bar under the
syllable and lights the syllable while it sounds, so you can follow the timing
by ear.

## A way to practise a new song

1. Find do. Play do, re, mi, fa, so, la, ti, do' in the song's key until it
   feels natural.
2. Listen to one line of the song while following the syllables.
3. Play that line slowly. In the player, set the speed to half.
4. Play along with the recording at normal speed, then move to the next line.
