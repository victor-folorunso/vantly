## Fixed do

In fixed-do solfège (often written solfege), do is always C, re is always D, and so on, whatever key
the song is in. The syllables are simply names for the notes. Fixed do is
common in France, Italy, Spain and much of Latin America.

## Movable do

In movable-do solfège, do is the home note of the song. In G, do is G; in F,
do is F. A tune is written with the same syllables in any key, so the
syllables tell you how each note feels in the key rather than its name.

## Tonic solfa

Tonic solfa is a movable-do system developed in England in the 1800s by Sarah
Glover and spread by John Curwen. It writes the syllables as letters (d r m f
s l t), marks high and low notes with apostrophes and commas, and has its own
way of writing rhythm with colons and bar lines. It became the standard
notation for choirs and churches across much of Africa and in parts of Asia.

## What the song to solfa tool writes

- Movable do: do is the home note of your song, and the player tells you which
  key it is.
- Minor songs are la-based: the home note of a minor song is la.
- Raised notes end in -i: di, ri, fi, si, li.
- The exact keyboard key under every syllable, so you are never left to work
  it out.
