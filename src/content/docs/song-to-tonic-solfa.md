Upload the song and wait a few seconds. The player opens straight away and the
solfa fills in part by part while the rest of the song is worked out, so you
can press play long before it has finished. Most music transcription software,
AI music transcription included, turns audio into sheet music on a staff. This
uses the same kind of AI but turns the song into tonic solfa instead, the
notation many singers and church keyboardists already read, with the key to
press written under every syllable.

## What happens to the song

1. The singer is separated from the instruments, so the band does not get
   mistaken for the tune.
2. A model trained on singing writes down each note the lead voice sings: when
   it starts, how long it lasts, and its pitch.
3. Each note is moved onto the nearest keyboard key. If the whole recording is
   slightly sharp or flat, or the singer drifts as they go, that is corrected
   first, so a song recorded a little off pitch still lands on real keys.
4. The key is worked out from the tune, and every note is written as a
   syllable relative to do.
5. The chords are read from the instruments and written above the solfa where
   they change, with their number in the key, like IV or V, for your left hand.

## Why solfa and not sheet music

Audio to sheet music tools write notes on a staff, which assumes you read staff
notation. A great many singers and keyboard players learned tonic solfa
instead, especially in church and school choirs across Nigeria, Ghana, South
Africa and Kenya. Solfa also shows how each note sits in the key, which is how
you actually hear a tune, so a song is easier to learn by ear in solfa than
from note names.

If you do read staff notation, switch the player to show it. The pitches are
the same ones the solfa shows. The rhythm is fitted to the beat of the
recording, which is an estimate, so the player lets you change the time
signature, halve or double the beat, and move the first beat until the bars
look right.

## What works well, and what does not

- **Best:** songs with one clear lead singer, recorded cleanly.
- **Harder:** fast runs, heavy vibrato and slides between notes. Slides are
  cleaned up to the note the singer was aiming for, and those syllables are
  underlined. Switch to the exact version to see what was really sung.
- **Not written out:** choir harmony parts and backing vocals. Only the lead
  voice is transcribed. Sections with no singing, like a keyboard intro, show
  their chords only.
- **Short clips** are harder to key correctly, because there is less of the
  tune to go on. If the key sounds wrong, change do in the settings and every
  syllable updates at once.

No automatic transcription is perfect, and this one is no exception. Treat the
result as a strong first draft and check it by ear: the player can play the
detected notes on a piano sound next to the original, or on their own.

## Taking it with you

Print the solfa, or save it as a PDF from the print dialog, which gives a clean
page with the title, the key and the syllables and nothing else. Download it as
plain text to paste into WhatsApp or a message to the choir. Download MIDI for
a keyboard app or recording software, or MusicXML for MuseScore, Sibelius,
Finale and Dorico. Every download uses the key and the settings you have on
screen, so moving the key names to C first gives you a copy in C.

## Your song

The song is sent to a server, because separating a voice and transcribing it
needs far more computing power than a browser has. It is kept there so the
player can play it back, and it is not listed anywhere public. Only someone
with the link can open it, and you can delete it from Your songs whenever you
like.
